# Haemostasis, Antithrombotics, & Transfusion Medicine

## Anticoagulants (Coagulation Cascade)

### Heparins (UFH & LMWH)

#### Mechanism

- Potentiate Antithrombin III (ATIII) affinity (~1000x)
- UFH: Inhibits Factor IIa (Thrombin) & Factor Xa (1:1 ratio)
- LMWH: Preferentially inhibits Factor Xa (2:1 to 4:1 ratio)

#### Pharmacokinetics & Monitoring

- UFH: Unpredictable dose response; Monitored by APTT
- LMWH: Predictable PK; High bioavailability; Longer half-life (4-7 hrs); Routine monitoring not required

#### Adverse Effects

- Haemorrhage (Dose dependent)
- Heparin-Induced Thrombocytopenia/Thrombosis (HITT Type II)
- Osteoporosis (Long term UFH use)
- Lower HIT risk with LMWH

#### Reversal

- UFH: Protamine Sulfate (Immediate, full reversal)
- LMWH: Protamine less reliable/incomplete

#### Special Use

- Preferred in pregnancy (does not cross placenta)
- Inhibits Vitamin K epoxide reductase (liver)
- Prevents gamma-carboxylation of Factors II, VII, IX, X
- Also inhibits Protein C & S (Anticoagulants)

### Vitamin K Antagonists (VKAs): Warfarin

#### Pharmacology & Risks

- Delayed onset/offset (36-72 hr half-life)
- Monitoring by International Normalized Ratio (INR)
- Bleeding risk increases exponentially with INR (>3)
- Interactions: Age, diet (Vitamin K), antibiotics, displacement (Aspirin)
- Teratogenicity/Embryopathy (Contraindicated in 1st trimester)

#### Reversal (Life-threatening Bleeding)

- First-line: 4-Factor PCC (Rapid INR correction, low volume)
- Adjunctive: Vitamin K (Slow, sustained effect)
- Secondary: Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP, slow, high volume/TACO risk)

### Direct Oral Anticoagulants (DOACs)

#### Agents & Mechanism

- Direct Thrombin Inhibitor: Dabigatran
- Direct Factor Xa Inhibitors (FXaI): Rivaroxaban, Apixaban, Edoxaban
- Predictable PK; Short half-lives; No routine monitoring required

#### Adverse Effects & Risk

- Lower risk of Intracranial Hemorrhage (ICH) vs. Warfarin
- Increased risk of GI bleeding (Rivaroxaban)

#### Reversal (Emergent)

- Dabigatran: Idarucizumab (Specific monoclonal antibody fragment)
- FXaI: Andexanet alfa (Specific recombinant FXa decoy protein)
- Non-Specific (Second-line): 4F-PCC (Off-label use)
- Risk: Reversal exposes patient to underlying thrombotic risk

#### Perioperative Management

- Cessation governed by CrCl (Renal Clearance) & bleeding risk
- Therapy resumption typically 48-72 hrs post-op (if hemostasis achieved)

## Antiplatelet Agents (Platelet Function)

### Aspirin (COX-1 Inhibitor)

- Mechanism: Irreversible inhibition of COX-1 via acetylation
- Effect: Suppresses Thromboxane A₂ (TXA₂) synthesis
- Duration: Lasts for platelet lifespan (7-10 days)

### ADP Receptor Inhibitors

- Agents: Clopidogrel, Ticlopidine
- Mechanism: Prodrugs irreversibly block ADP receptor (P2Y₁₂)
- Effect: Prevents activation of GPIIb/IIIa receptor
- Adverse Effects: Bleeding, Neutropenia (Ticlopidine > Clopidogrel)

### GPIIb/IIIa Antagonists

- Agents: Abciximab, Tirofiban, Eptifibatide
- Mechanism: Block final common pathway (Fibrinogen binding)
- Use: IV in Acute Coronary Syndromes (ACS) or PCI

## Transfusion Medicine: Principles & Risks

### Blood Group Compatibility

#### ABO System

- Antigens (A, B) on RBC surface; Antibodies (Anti-A, Anti-B) in plasma (IgM)
- Universal RBC Donor: O Negative Packed Red Cells (PRBCs) (Lacks A/B/D antigens)
- Universal Plasma Donor: AB Fresh Frozen Plasma (FFP) (Lacks Anti-A/B antibodies)
- Risk: High-titer anti-A/B in Group O plasma (can cause Haemolysis in non-O recipients)

#### Rhesus (Rh) System

- Antigens (D protein) are highly immunogenic
- Anti-D antibodies are IgG (not naturally occurring)
- RhD Negative females of child-bearing age must receive RhD negative products
- Weak D/Partial D: Requires Rh-negative cells to prevent alloimmunisation

#### Compatibility Testing (3 Steps)

- Blood Typing (Forward/Reverse Grouping)
- Antibody Screen (Detects non-ABO IgG antibodies, e.g., Kell, Kidd, Duffy)
- Crossmatch (Final in-vitro compatibility check)

### Transfusion Reactions (TRs)

#### Acute Immunological (<24 hrs)

##### Acute Haemolytic TR (AHTR)

- Cause: ABO incompatibility (Clerical error most common)
- Mechanism: IgM activation of Classical Complement Pathway, MAC formation
- Consequences: Shock/Hypotension, DIC, Acute Renal Failure (Free Hb scavenging NO)
- Management: Stop Tx, IV Fluids/Inotropes, Renal protection, Antibiotics (if sepsis suspected)

##### Other Acute Immunological Reactions

- Febrile Non-Haemolytic TR (FNHTR): Fever/chills; Cytokines released from donor WBCs
- Transfusion-Related Acute Lung Injury (TRALI): Non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema, Normal BNP, Diuretics contraindicated
- Anaphylaxis (Severe Allergic): IgA deficiency / Anti-IgA antibodies

#### Delayed (>24 hrs)

- Delayed Haemolytic TR (DHTR): Anamnestic response to minor antigens (Kidd, Duffy, Kell)
- Transfusion-Associated Graft-Versus-Host Disease (TA-GVHD): Viable donor T-cells engrafting in immunocompromised host; Prevented by irradiation

#### Non-Immunological

- Transfusion-Related Sepsis: Platelets highest risk (Stored at 20-24°C)
- Transfusion-Associated Circulatory Overload (TACO): Cardiogenic oedema (Volume overload); Elevated BNP

## Massive Transfusion Protocol (MTP)

### Goals & Activation

- Activation: ABC≥2 or rapid administration of 4+ units PRBCs in 4 hrs
- Primary Goal: Arrest bleeding, maintain perfusion, dismantle Lethal Triad
- Component Ratio: Balanced resuscitation (e.g., PRBC:FFP:Platelets 1:1:1)

### Lethal Triad

- Hypothermia (Goal >35°C): Impairs clotting factor activity
- Acidosis (Goal pH >7.2): Worsens coagulopathy
- Coagulopathy: Dilutional effects, factor consumption

### Complications of Massive Transfusion

#### Storage Lesion Effects

- Hyperkalaemia (K+ leakage; Risk of arrhythmias)
- Hypocalcaemia (Citrate toxicity; Impairs clotting factors & myocardial function)
- Impaired Oxygen Delivery (2,3-DPG decline causes Left ODC shift)

#### Haemostatic Impairment

- Dilutional Coagulopathy (Lack of factors/platelets in PRBCs)
- Fibrinogen depletion (Requires early Cryoprecipitate replacement; Goal >1.0 g/L)
